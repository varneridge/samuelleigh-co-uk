I chose the degree for the international side rather than the historical one. Most of what I
have taken sits where foreign policy, economic structure and institutions meet, which is also
what I write about.

### First year, 2024/25

- **Empires and Resistance in Global History.**
  Imperial systems and the movements that contested them. This was where I first took the
  politics of sources seriously.
- **International Politics since 1914: Peace and War.**
  The standard survey, and useful scaffolding for everything since.
- **Historical Approaches to the Modern World.**
  Historiography and method. My essay on the Institute of Race Relations archive came out of
  this one.
- **Microeconomics I and Macroeconomics I.**
  The quantitative half of the degree.
- **LSE100: How can we create a fair society?**

### Second year, 2025/26

- **The International History of the Cold War, 1945 to 1989.**
  The source of my essay arguing that the Sino-Soviet split was a choice rather than a
  structural inevitability.
- **The United States and the World since 1776.**
  Where I wrote on economics as both the engine of American territorial consolidation and the
  threat to it.
- **Napoleon and Europe.**
  Economic structures and the mechanics of continental hegemony.
- **International Organisations.**
  How multilateral institutions are designed, and why design choices persist long after the
  reason for them has gone.
