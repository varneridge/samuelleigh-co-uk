Elisabeth Braw proposed training civilian volunteers as jointing assistants to supplement the
professional crews who repair undersea cables, on the model of the military reserves. That is
structurally the RNLI: civilian volunteers maintaining critical national infrastructure
alongside professionals, at a fraction of the cost of holding the same capacity in
full-time employment.

You are one of those volunteers. So the piece writes itself in a way it would not for anyone
else in the room: what the model actually requires to work, what it costs in training hours
that nobody counts, why retention is the hard part rather than recruitment, and what happens to
readiness when the volunteers are two hours from the station.

The session's framing question, from Liam Byrne, was whether the country is at peace or not.
