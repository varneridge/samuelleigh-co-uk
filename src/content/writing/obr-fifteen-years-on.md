The figure worth building the piece around is that the average shelf life of a UK fiscal rule
over the past decade has been 1.8 years, and that no single mandate has survived to its target
date in outturn. That is a fact about the rules, not about any one Chancellor.

Other threads worth pulling on:

- The OBR forecasting record against the Treasury: more accurate in years one to three, wider
  of the mark in years four and five. All of the borrowing miss in the later years is
  attributable to departmental spending assumptions.
- Productivity as a residual, and successive downgrades from the financial crisis through the
  pandemic to structural factors including reduced trade openness.
- Dynamic scoring and the 0.1 per cent of GDP significance threshold, which took nineteen
  scored policies down to six material ones, with the asymmetry that the Treasury presents
  beneficial indirect effects and not the other kind.
- The Fiscal Risk and Sustainability report getting almost no attention despite being, as it
  was put, a forecast for the next fifty years.
- Hughes on the IFS traffic light proposal, and why he thought it would enable target-shopping.

Note the context honestly: this was Hughes's first Commons appearance since resigning over the
early disclosure of the EFO in November 2025.
